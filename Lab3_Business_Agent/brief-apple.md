# Apple (AAPL) — CFO Business Briefing

## Audience
For a CFO assessing equity exposure, pricing power, operating risk, and cash requirements.

## Requested Window and Actual Price Observations
August 1–October 31, 2026. The 64 daily observations run from August 3 through October 30; requested endpoints were Saturdays.

## Prepared As Of
November 14, 2026, America/New_York; a retrospective assessment of the requested period.

## Price Source
Yahoo Finance [P1], cross-checked against Nasdaq [P2]. All 64 dates and closing prices match after consistent cent rounding.

## Price Basis
Unadjusted daily closing prices in USD, rounded half up to cents. Results measure price appreciation rather than total shareholder return.

## Price Performance
AAPL rose from **$303.42** to **$344.63**, gaining **$41.21**, or **13.58%**. Python calculated `(344.63 / 303.42 - 1) × 100` from the [saved observations](apple/prices-used.csv). The [daily chart](apple-price-chart.png) includes every observed session; the endpoint gain does not eliminate intervening risk.

## Two Developments
1. **October 27 — product-quality exposure:** RTTNews reported that Apple would replace iPhone Duo units affected by inner-display problems [N1]. The article quotes Apple describing a “small number” of affected devices but supplies no unit count or cost estimate. **CFO relevance:** monitor warranty provisions, replacement logistics, and customer-retention costs; the saved evidence cannot quantify a financial impact.
2. **October 29 — premium pricing:** Patrick Sanders, writing for The Motley Fool, reported touchscreen MacBook Pro starting prices of **$2,899** and **$3,399**, with a **$400** premium over comparable same-chip models [N2]. **CFO relevance:** assess whether premium product mix can offset component costs without weakening demand. Higher selling prices alone do not establish higher margins; no margin improvement is assumed.

## What It May Mean
**Interpretation:** Review valuation assumptions alongside product-quality exposure and premium-pricing execution. The operational developments identify questions for financial planning, not evidence that either event caused the share-price gain. This briefing makes no investment recommendation.

## Two Limitations
1. Closing-price returns exclude dividends, intraday risk, and benchmark comparisons.
2. News evidence is secondary reporting; replacement costs and realized product margins remain unverified. Apple's newsroom was inaccessible.
